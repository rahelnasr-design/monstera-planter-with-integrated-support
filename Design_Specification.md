# Integrated Central-Support Monstera Planter

## Design intent

This concept solves the instability of a loose plant stake placed directly in soil. The support pole is concentric with the planter and seats in a blind structural socket that is integrated into the planter base. Excess irrigation water drains directly into a removable saucer; there is no permanent internal water reservoir.

## Functional architecture

1. **Central blind socket** — keeps the pole on the exact planter axis and prevents soil or drainage water from entering the pole cavity from below.
2. **360-degree conical gusset** — transfers pole bending loads symmetrically into the planter floor while avoiding direction-specific ribs.
3. **Direct drainage** — six holes outside the gusset allow excess water to pass from the soil into the saucer.
4. **Raised saucer support** — the planter sits above collected drainage water instead of standing in it.
5. **Removable saucer** — enables emptying and cleaning without a hidden standing-water chamber.
6. **Low ballast ring** — fits into the annular underside recess and lowers the product centre of gravity.
7. **Wide circular footprint** — provides equal resistance to tipping in every direction.

## Suggested materials

- Planter and saucer: UV-stabilized polypropylene for production; PETG for a large-format prototype.
- Support pole: anodized aluminium tube, sealed hardwood dowel, or glass-filled polymer.
- Ballast: powder-coated carbon steel or stainless steel.

## Water path

Water enters from the soil surface, percolates through the growing medium, exits through the six floor holes, and collects in the removable saucer. The saucer should be emptied after drainage. The design intentionally avoids a self-watering reservoir to reduce root-rot, stagnant-water, insect, and cleaning risks.

## Validation before production

- Confirm the chosen pole-to-socket clearance with a printed prototype.
- Perform a loaded tilt test with wet soil and a representative mature plant.
- Verify drainage flow using the intended soil mix and a removable mesh over the holes.
- Add draft angles, wall-thickness checks, fillets, and tooling splits if injection molding is selected.
- Confirm ballast retention with a snap cover or fastened bottom plate before manufacture.
